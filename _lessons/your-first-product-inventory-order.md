## **Introduction**

Now that you have found a product opportunity, found a supplier, and designed your label and packaging, it is time to place your first order for inventory.  

**Here are a few things to remember and consider when placing your first inventory order:**

-   **Minimize Your Risk** 

When placing your first inventory order, and even the subsequent orders, you'll want to order as little as possible. This is to reduce the financial risk you must take in order to invest in this product.

Because you've found a product opportunity that's already selling well, selling on Amazon is less risky than selling elsewhere, but you still want to minimize your risk. If you sell quickly, this may mean ordering less than you sell, which means you will most likely run out of inventory.

Later on, making sure you don't run out of inventory will be a big part of inventory management, but for now, that's fine. In fact, it's a positive sign because it indicates that you've sold a large number of items.

-   **How Long to Get Your Product** 

You should know how long it takes your supplier to prepare your product for shipment from the moment you order it. This will help you plan your purchasing and it's another way to compare suppliers. 

-   **Cash Flow** 

Cash flow is important in this type of business because if you buy your product today, and your supplier takes a month to prepare it, your cash is all tied up in that, so you want suppliers who minimize how long they tie up your cash.  

The following is an example of the timeline involved:  

-   -   -   You place your order 
        -   The supplier takes a couple of days to prepare your shipment 
        -   Your order is shipped to FBA (Fulfillment By Amazon) 
        -   Once received by FBA, it may take up to 7 days to process 
        -   At that point you start receiving revenue in your Seller Central account, and then after your  14 day payoff cutoff time, you’ll receive that money back.

As far as the question of how to position yourself in regards to your cash flow, let's consider this: if you purchase your product using credit cards, which are typically due every 30 days, and your product revenue returns take a couple of weeks, you're in a decent position. I am not giving financial advice here, but this is how I operate and it works for me. 

-   **Managing Inventory** 

When it comes to managing your inventory and ordering products, remember - **it is completely your responsibility**. It doesn't matter whether your supplier has the world's best account manager or if you talked to a very helpful person at Amazon, none of them will help you manage your inventory. **You need to take responsibility for that** and stay on top of it **every single day** while you’re building this up. Eventually, you may be able to delegate it or, if you continue to do it yourself, you will have an idea of what you need to do in order to keep those products in stock. 

Once the opportunity is proven and your business is up and running, and sales are coming in consistently, you can place larger inventory orders. This allows you to stay in stock, but it will also allow you to make more money. Every supplier operates under the model _**"the more you buy, the bigger the discount"**._ It means less risk since you know it's selling well and the more units you sell, the more money you make.

## **Fulfilment by Amazon (FBA)**

The purpose of this lesson is to discuss the use of FBA (Fulfillment by Amazon) to automatically ship your products to your customers. What you need to focus on now is how to get your product to your customers. I recommend using Amazon's fulfilment system called _FBA (_[_Fulfillment by Amazon_](https://sell.amazon.com/fulfillment-by-amazon)_)._ 

### **What is FBA?**

FBA is a service provided by Amazon that assists sellers with the storage and shipping of their products to customers. Amazon will let you ship inventory to them so they can store it, and orders placed through Amazon will be fulfilled automatically from that inventory. Your Seller Central dashboard lets you see how much inventory you have on hand, so you'll always know when you need to reorder.

### **Benefits of Using FBA** 

**1\. Boost in Product Ranking**  

By doing your own fulfilment and switching to FBA, your product virtually immediately moves up the rankings. 

**2\. Handle your Fulfillment Process**

In FBA, Amazon handles all your shipping and almost all your customer service. If someone has a question about their order, it is directed to Amazon's customer service, not yours. Amazon will track their order and help with those types of things. Automation allows you to focus on the other aspects of this training, such as marketing your product and getting traffic. 

#### **3\. Reasonably priced**

Your product will be eligible for Amazon Prime if you use FBA. Not only that, but Amazon has partnered with several of the world's leading shipping companies, lowering costs.

#### **4\. No Need to Worry about your Product Warehouse**

Every business owner requires a location to store their product. Small or large, expensive or not. Whether or not you have already budgeted for it, it will cause a lot of hassle in the long run, especially when it comes to managing it. But don't worry, Fulfillment by Amazon offers their fulfilment centres. You will be given the right space for your products.

You may already have your own e-commerce store; you may already accept orders, store them, and ship them. You might already be working with a fulfilment company. That's fine, but there are numerous advantages to using FBA for your Amazon Business.

## **Quickly Create an Amazon Product Listing**

In this lesson, you'll learn how to set up a quick Amazon product listing using Amazon Seller Central with only the most basic information. 

For the time being, we only require the basics. You may be wondering, **"WHY ONLY THE BASIC?”** There are a few reasons why you should only use basic, required information when creating your Amazon product listing at this point.

### **To Prepare an FBA Shipment** 

The first reason is that you'll be able to prepare an FBA shipment this way. This would not be possible without a listing. It's time to place your first inventory order now that you've found your supplier. When your supplier is ready to ship your inventory to Amazon, you'll need to prepare an FBA shipment and send those PDFs to them so they can apply them (one outside the box, one inside the box). This will ensure that those inventory packages are delivered directly to Amazon's warehouses and that the inventory count is automatically updated in your account, allowing you to begin receiving sales.

Don't worry if you don't understand what this means right now; it will be explained in greater depth in the next lesson. For the time being, just concentrate on getting your Amazon product listing up and running quickly.

### **Start Your Action!** 

You might be concerned about optimizing your listing. It is not necessary to have complete optimization right away. Remember, the goal is to get you up and running as quickly as possible.

Some suppliers may take a couple of weeks to prepare for some people and products, while others may only take a couple of days or even one day. This is dependent on the product, the supplier, and the requirements for labelling and packaging.

Whatever the case may be, we want to ensure that you are ready when the supplier is, which is why we only need to do a quick listing setup in Seller Central for your Amazon product. 

### **Inside your Amazon Seller Central Account** 

To set up your Amazon product listing, follow these simple steps: 

**1.Log in to your Amazon Seller Central Account.**

**2\. Go to the Inventory menu and select “Add product” located at the top right corner of the page.** 

**3\. Select “Create a new product listing”. Select the category.** 

There is no perfect method to this.  The best approach is to select the top-level category for the top-ranking product you discovered and for which you're creating a similar product.

For example, if I were selling a "cat brush," I would look in the '**Pet Supplies**' category. Then I'd choose a sub-category, which was '**Cat Supplies**.' As a result, deeper sub-categories will be displayed. 

It's fine if you can't find an exact sub-category as long as the top-level category matches the top-ranking product you're matching with. Click "Select Category" once you've found the category you want.

**4\. Enter your product info.** 

When you click "Select Category" for your product category, you're taken to our page, which contains a number of tabs of information fields for you to fill out. The information you see will differ depending on the product category. This information will be edited/optimized later, but for now, you only need to enter the mandatory information \[marked with a red asterisk (\*) and red box\]. This information isn't necessarily going to be the final value, but that's okay because you can edit it at any time.

When you turn on the Advanced View the following is brief information on the various fields you will fill in:

**A. “Vital Info” tab**

**I. Product Name** 

For the product name, just enter whatever product name you’ve come up with; e.g. _Blue Cat Brush._

**II. Manufacturer**

You can use whatever your brand name is. We discussed in the previous lesson how you can decide which and what brand name you should use for your product.

**III. UPC or EAN** 

The UPC is the same thing as your barcode.

**B. “Offer” tab**

**I. Condition** 

Select “New”.

**II. Your price** 

This is your selling price. You can change this any time you want. so don’t worry about getting it “right”. 

**III. Quantity** 

You don’t really need to worry about a specific number as you’re using FBA, but you do need to enter something. “100” is good enough.

**IV. Shipping Method** 

Make sure “I want Amazon to ship and provide customer service for my items if they sell” is selected.

**C. “Images” tab** 

Don’t do anything with images right now. 

**D. “Description” tab** 

Only enter what Amazon requires. E

**E.** **“Keywords” tab** 

Only enter what Amazon requires. 

**F. “More Details” tab** 

Only enter what Amazon requires.

Take note that when filling out the required information, make sure to adhere to the Amazon Quality Standard. Like:

-   -   -   -   Properly formatted product title.
            -   Images with a high resolution and a white background.
            -   Product Description in Detail

This will aid Amazon customers' purchasing decisions.

As soon as you've entered all of the required information, the **"Save and Finish"** button, which appears below the information fields on each tab, will become active. Once you click, you've done all that is required at this point in the training.  

Now, depending on the product, there may be some required fields not mentioned here. For those fields, just make something up. If you’re not really sure about it, don’t start sending up support tickets unless there’s something that’s a real deal-breaker question. 

## **FBA Online Shipment Preparation**

You'll learn how to prepare an FBA shipment so that your supplier can send your inventory directly to Amazon's warehouses in this lesson.

Some suppliers may be unfamiliar with selling to an Amazon entrepreneur because they are accustomed to shipping to an office, a warehouse, or even a residence, but they will be sending the shipment directly to Amazon's warehouses in this case. Because Amazon is such a large company, many people are familiar with this, but if they aren't, it's very simple and should be simple to show them how to do it. 

### **FBA Shipment**  

There are 3 simple fundamentals involved when thinking about the FBA Shipment process: 

-   -   **Suppliers can ship inventory directly to FBA.**
        

Instead of going through a third-party fulfilment company, you can have your supplier send your inventory directly to Amazon's warehouses, saving you a lot of time and money. 

-   -   **Shipping documents are provided by FBA Setup.**
        

Whenever you set up an FBA product, Amazon generates a PDF that you send to your supplier and have them attach to the outside of each shipping carton (there used to be two, but recently they changed it to just one). This document is important because it informs Amazon what products are inside each box and where they need to be shipped. 

-   -   **Supplier hands-off box to UPS**  
        

If the supplier does not or cannot do UPS shipping, you can either:  

-   -   -   -   Schedule a pick-up at their facility. 
                
            -   Use whatever shipping method they use.  
                

I will show how to use another shipping method in the example below.

#### **Set Up and Use Prepared FBA Shipment**

The following steps will show you how to make an Amazon FBA shipment: 

**1\. Within your Seller Central dashboard, go to “Manage FBA Inventory”** a. Log in to Seller Central, then go to Inventory => Manage FBA Inventory.

**2\. Find your product**

**a.** When you're new to Amazon, there might be just one. Check the box next to the product you're handling, then click on **"Send/Replenish Inventory"** from the dropdown list. 

**3\. Create New Shipping Plan** 

**a.** Make sure you are shipping the products to the correct address. If not, edit the address. 

**b.** Be sure to pick "Case-packed products" 

**c.** Select "Continue with shipping plan" 

**d.** Enter the number of units per case

**e.** Enter the number of cases you are shipping

**f.** Select “Continue” 

**g**. On the "Prepare Products" screen, you can select "Continue" unless you are asking Amazon to do any prep work, such as poly bagging. This will likely be skipped.

**h.** On the "Label Products" screen, you can have Amazon add the FNSKU stickers for you (they usually only cost $ 0.30 per item) or you can print them and attach them manually. While this screen is titled "Label Products," it is actually meant only to add the FNSKU sticker as part of the label for your product (NOT the one that shows the product name, directions, etc.). 

**i.** On the “Review Shipments” screen, just select “Approve Shipments”. Please note that Amazon may have split your shipment up into multiple shipments going to multiple FBA warehouses. This is normal and helps to make sure Amazon gets your products to your customers as quickly as possible.

**j.** On the following screen, select “Work on shipment”

**k.** On the “Prepare Shipment” screen, you will enter the details of your shipment, such as the size and weight of the box(es). 

**l.** Keep the shipment method as “Small Parcel Delivery” and keep the shipping carrier as “Amazon-Preferred Carrier (UPS)” 

**m.** Enter the shipping dimensions of your package that will contain all of your products. 

**n.** Important: If this particular shipment will contain multiple boxes going to this warehouse, you will want to hit “Copy Last Box” once the dimensions of the first box have been entered until the appropriate number of boxes shows up. 

**o.** Select “Calculate” to see the estimated shipping price

**p.** Select the “I agree” box and then “Accept charges”

**q.** Select “Print box labels” and save the pdf files to your computer. 

This shipment is now complete and ready to ship. You can select **"Marked as Shipped"** once you know your supplier has shipped your inventory. It's not required, but it makes it easier for you to keep track of what's going on in your Seller Central account. Simply return to the 'Manage FBA Inventory' screen to see when your inventory is in stock and available, and you'll know when the shipment has arrived in the Amazon warehouses.
