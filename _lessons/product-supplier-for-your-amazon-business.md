If it's ok to be more expensive, how do we determine the price point? Are we testing to see what the market will bear?**

## **Step 4: Using the Potential Product Finder System to Find Supplier**

-   **How close can we come to the design of an existing product without"infringing?" e.g., one of the items I'm interested in shows "designed by …", but doesn't show any patent, trademark, or anything else.** 

The most important thing is to make your product stand out compared to the competition so you don't want to copy them too close. 

-   **If I do not live in the same region as the Amazon marketplace I'm looking to operate in (e.g. US/Amazon.com, UK/amazon.co.uk), how do I make sure Google searches look in the region I am wanting to sell in?** 

To keep from having Google default to the region you are in (e.g. US), go to http://www.google.com/search?gl=us then type in the search as normal. This should provide you with the US results. 

-   **Does each "site:url" have a space between it?**

Yes, but make sure there is no space between the colon and the URL. 

-   **Should there be a space after 'private label' and before 'site:url'?** 

Yes, there is space. 

-   **How do we ensure the same quality of a product that we read reviews for on a site like Amazon without a good review from a supplier?**

If you have questions regarding the quality, you can ask for a sample. 

-   **Would you want to include any overseas suppliers at this point?** 

While starting with a local supplier is easier in terms of communication, your options for variety will be severely limited. It is recommended that you investigate all product options in order to obtain the best price for the highest quality product possible.

-   **How much longer would delivery from an overseas supplier?** 

Believe it or not, delivery from a foreign supplier does not have to be any slower than from a domestic supplier. If the supplier has the product in stock, it can ship it to the United States in 2 to 3 days (especially if the product is lightweight). For bulkier items, shipping by air freight will take 1–2 weeks, and shipping by sea will take 4-6 weeks. The more quickly you require the product, the more expensive that type of shipping will be. 

-   **If we do go the overseas route (e.g. China) for our first product, what would an aggressive (but realistic) time frame be to profitability?** 

It all depends on the product and how quickly the overseas company can prepare it (and how fast you move). Some products ordered from outside the United States arrive faster than those ordered from within the United States, while others take longer.

-   **How can we protect ourselves from the manufacturer going around us and selling directly to Amazon?** 

The nature of the Supplier business model, which is the supply of products to other companies rather than the general public, is typically considered sufficient grounds for not being concerned, as mentioned in the training.

-   **Are we only looking for US-­­based manufacturers or suppliers or are they the same?** 

It's all about getting the best deal, so you'll want to look around as much as possible. It's best if you can find the cheapest supplier in the United States, but this isn't always the case.

-   **Do we look for the exact product and try to private label that one or do we find a similar product to private label?** 

You're on the lookout for a similar item. Finding the exact product to private label is extremely difficult, and impossible if the Amazon seller is the manufacturer.

-   **If we do find the exact product, should we go with that and try to beat it out?** 

Yes, absolutely, but make sure you give your product a different look to the other existing product. 

-   **Shouldn't the private label also be in quotes, and shouldn't there be a plus between your search terms to indicate that you want a site that mentions both/all of them? E.g. "olive oil sprayer" + "private label".** 

Do it in any way you want to get the desired results. Experiment with different variations to see what works best for you. 

-   **The training says that when we look for suppliers, we should be digging a few pages deep. However, in the video, all the data gathered was from page 1. Do we really have to dig a little more than that?** 

The video demonstrates how to do it, but it is constrained in order to keep the video brief and to the point. You'll want to dig a little deeper to make sure you've found the right suppliers to contact.

## **Using Alibaba.com to Find Suppliers and Sourcing Product**

-   **What payment method is the best, as it seems most of them don't accept PayPal payment?** 

Most will initially want a money transfer. It is not uncommon to expect to pay 30% upfront, then the remainder when the product is ready to be shipped. 

-   **I have not seen any suppliers on Alibaba that list credit cards as a payment type. How do you find suppliers that accept credit cards?** 

It's possible that you'll be able to negotiate a credit card payment method with a supplier after your initial purchase. This has happened with some US suppliers, but even if the supplier is from the US, it is not guaranteed. You might want to look into Alibaba.com's escrow service, which not only allows you to pay with a credit card but also adds a layer of security when dealing with an unknown supplier for the first time. There's a lot more information about it there.

-   **When trying to get quotes, I'm asked for a destination port. I have Amazon's FBA address but don't know the destination port. Where do I find it?** 

The port of destination will be determined by who you use to receive the inventory at the port, not Amazon.

-   **One supplier says, "Our min order is 100 pcs and the fee to ship to the USA per pc is 5.14USD". Does that mean the total price shipped to FBA, or to a port? When we send them the shipping labels to FBA, does the supplier make sure it gets all the way there?** 

It would be best to send them an FBA address (just search online for one in the US) and see what they say. 

-   **Is it okay to order from a Chinese supplier without getting a sample first?** 

This is not recommended. Get a sample first to ensure you are getting the product you are paying for. 

-   **When opening an Alibaba account they ask for a business location. I'm from the UK but selling on Amazon.com so should I say US? It also asks if I'm a supplier or buyer... selling on Amazon would make me a supplier?**

Give your real location. 

-   **If a supplier doesn't have a product FDA approved, is it best to move on to another supplier? Just wondering about the time and cost of having to do that yourself.** 

It depends on the type of product and the certification. There are other certifications that can be obtained. 

-   **Will a supplier from Alibaba ship your items directly to Amazon? If they do, I'm assuming you have to send the supplier your FBA shipping materials, or you should have them send your items to you and you ship the items to Amazon?** 

Yes, they will, and you will send the packing slips to them. You should use DHL and obtain shipping quotes from both DHL and the supplier (they can often get a cheaper rate).

-   **You said, "When working with Alibaba, I found that two things happen. #1) You have to contact them more than once sometimes." You never really specified the second thing.** 

The second thing to remember is that in order to get better responses, you must present yourself in a professional manner.

-   **Can I put my own labels on products I find on Alibaba?** 

Yes, you can send them your label design and have them print it for you. Many of them also offer design and printing services in-house.

-   **The keywords/products that I am going after do not have any assessed suppliers on Alibaba. Are they still safe to deal with or should I avoid them?** 

If no good suppliers appear at first, start with all filters and then back out as needed. They should be fine; it's not as if one item means everyone else is a con artist; it's just an optional extra step.

-   **If we do manage to get suppliers in the US, does Amazon FBA actually fetch the product from the supplier, so I don't have to worry about the shipment as Amazon takes care of it?** 

All you have to do is print label PDFs from Seller Central and email them to the supplier. They hand the boxes with labels to the shipment company. And that's it. 

**Contacting Suppliers: What to Say, What Information to Gather, & How to Get Setup** 

-   **Do we need to cover UPC symbols with the potential supplier? For instance, I talked to a supplier that already has a UPC symbol for the product I'm interested in, and they will not take mine. Is this okay or do I need to consider a different supplier?**

When selling your OWN product/brand as recommended, you will need your own UPC - you can't possibly get a UPC from your supplier. 

-   **What is a UPC?** 

"UPC" stands for "Universal Product Code", and is a unique 12 digit number represented by a barcode. 

-   **Have you ever encountered a supplier who asks for a business name (and possibly your tax number, which you said “..tell them you don't have one.") before they will do business with you? If so, what would be the best thing to tell them if we don't have one or both at this point?** 

Ask them what else they'll accept and why they need it. Tell them you'll be paying upfront with a credit card, so you should not have any problems. 

-   **Do you control the test order by watching it yourself or do you let the supplier send the first order direct to the Amazon warehouse?** 

To keep things simple, I have my supplier send all orders directly to Amazon's warehouse via FBA. When the supplier won't take photos of the product, you'll need to request a sample.

-   **When you get the price from the supplier, will it include shipping? How do we know how to calculate our shipping cost from the supplier since we don't know the location of the FBA center where the supplier will be shipping our product?** 

It should be possible to get an estimated shipping cost based on how many units you order. You'll definitely know shipping costs once your units ship out because most suppliers won't cover the shipping. 

-   **Is it a good idea to get a sample first, for such things as checking the quality of the product, taking photos for my Amazon product listing, etc.?** 

Yes, you can ask for a sample, and most suppliers will send you one if you pay for delivery. 

-   **Is it a good idea to get samples from multiple suppliers?** 

If you find a couple of different suppliers who look like viable options, it might make sense to order samples and compare their quality. 

-   **I don’t have a company yet. Usually, when dealing with suppliers, they ask you to fill out forms and the first one is 'Company Name'. Will it make me look unprofessional if I say I’m buying as an individual?** 

I have a company name yet, simply use your own. I've been using my own name for a long time and have had no problems. Also, take a look at the videos on how to create your own domain email address and website. These will help you present a more professional image to any potential suppliers.

-   **Phone preference over email: Leaving aside time differences and call costs, is it practical to speak to suppliers in China or do they prefer mail due to possible language problems? Do suppliers ever use Skype?** 

While email is great for initial introductions and gathering information, you'll want to move on to Skype, Whatsapp, or the phone for more interactive communication. Even if communicating is difficult at first, you will want to master it because communication will be an important part of your relationship in the future. Just remember that they are people just like you, and they are trying their hardest to communicate with you as well.

-   **We don't have to arrange any of the shipping (other than the FBA info we provide them) -­­ the supplier will handle that?** 

That is correct for products being shipped within the United States. The shipping documents will be generated by you and emailed to your supplier, who will then attach them to your packages. You'll need to coordinate international shipments with your supplier and possibly a third-party shipping company/freight forwarder.

-   **I have had experience ordering some items from China where the first shipment from the supplier is good quality, but subsequent shipments are not. How do we know that the supplier will always ship with the same and good quality of the items to Amazon since we are not able to check physically?** 

Slowly ramp up the amounts and do product spot checks along the way (e.g., get a few products for you and a few for Amazon FBA). 

-   **When you say send FBA details to a supplier, is that by email or post?** 

Send the PDFs via email. More information on this can be found in the FBA lesson "Your First Product Inventory Order -­­ Part 4: FBA Online Shipment Preparation," which also includes an email template you can use to send the PDFs to the supplier.

-   **Suppliers for my first three picks are telling me they won’t have products ready for at least 60 to 90 days. I remember in the video it was stated to just find another product choice. Is that correct?** 

You do not want to use a supplier or product that has such a long turnaround time. 

-   **On Alibaba, do you always have to pay the whole amount up front?** 

You can negotiate that. Most overseas suppliers will accept 30% of the order when it is placed and the rest when the product is ready to ship. If you're interested, you can also look into Alibaba's escrow services.

-   **Do our products need barcodes?** 

To be set up on Amazon, all products must have a "UPC." Some products are also required by Amazon to have the UPC printed on them (depending on the category), so to be safe, we recommend including your UPC code in the design of your product label.

-   **When checking out suppliers, prices are shown as -­­-­­FOB Price: US $2.8-­­3.2 / Piece. I'm not quite sure what that is telling me. Does anybody have an idea?** 

FOB stands for _"Free On Board,"_ which means the supplier will cover the costs to a specific port and the buyer will cover the rest. Most overseas suppliers will usually quote you a price to get it to their port and then ship it the rest of the way at your expense. This is a common situation.

-   **I have found a supplier in China and they want to send me samples of their product ASAP. They are asking for an account number of UPS for freight collection. How do I go about getting one?** 

You can open a UPS account for free by going to UPS.com. 

-   **I have contacted a potential supplier via Alibaba who cannot offer me the smaller quantities as an initial order. They referred me to their wholesale site where I can purchase as little as I want. However, they cannot put my brand/logo on the products. My question: Is there a way I can ship to Amazon without a brand name and get Amazon to put it on once it arrives at the Amazon warehouse?** 

You could ship it to yourself so you can apply the labels and then ship it to FBA if you're ordering a small quantity to test the product on Amazon or because the funds required to fund the supplier's larger order are insufficient. Obviously, you'll need to design and print the label. I don't believe you can send items to Amazon that aren't labeled.

-   **With the supplier, I found I was able to get a sample order of 300 pcs. However, they do not do private labels on orders under 3000. Should I order the 300 and give it a try without my labels or look for another supplier?** 

Find another supplier. A product with someone else's brand on it shouldn't be added to Amazon as you will have to create a new listing when it is private-labeled. 

-   **What is ‘net 30’?** 

After ordering, you pay the supplier 30 days later. 

-   **I found a supplier in Beijing. I need some help understanding what some of the initials mean before I respond to a follow-­­up email with my contact: Payment terms: L/C, T/, B/G and Brand name: OEM**

It might be best to ask them directly or do a quick search using the resource you used to find them (e.g., Alibaba). OEM, for example, typically means "Owner End Manufacturer" but can also be used to describe a supplier. 

-   **If I want to have my first shipment come to my house and not direct to Amazon because I want to make sure the product is good, do I need to send them my FBA packing slip to go in the box and the outside?** 

If you have the shipment shipped to your home, you won't be using FBA for that. You will use FBA to transfer the shipment from your home to Amazon, so you will only use FBA labels when sending the items to Amazon. 

-   **Do suppliers offer guarantees to us regarding the product quality, especially overseas suppliers? Should we ask them about guarantees, or would that make us look like novices who don't understand the system?** 

They don't. That's why you should test. 

## **Step 5 of the Potential Product Finder System: Selecting One Opportunity**

If you choose the one you want, contact the other one to see if they will improve their offer. 

-   **OK! I have a product, but the suppliers all say I have to do my Private Label in the USA because they don't do it, they only sell cheap. There is no quantity limit, which is nice for me. Does Amazon have a limit on how much to put up on their website?** 

The most inventory you can add initially with FBA is 4000 or 5000 units, which is plenty of room to get started with.

## **Selecting a Product Name and a Brand Name**

-   **I am a bit confused about the purpose of my company brand versus product Branding/naming. For example, I could have my company brand as “Kreative Kitchens” and then one of my product brands as “SuperSensations Spatulas” under that company brand. In other words, will we be building mainly the product Brand or the corporate Brand?**

Corporate brands frequently begin with a product. Consider Coca-Cola, which began as a single product and has grown into a multi-product corporation.

-   **I already have a domain that relates to my product, however, it does not have the exact keyword, and just wondering if I could have a subdomain added to that site that has the product keyword in it?**

It is not necessary to create a subdomain. It is sufficient if the website is related. To promote my first product, I used about ten related blogs.

-   **I have the product I like, a supplier/ manufacturer, and am working on the brand. Have easily found a couple of very appropriate brand names with associated .com domains available. What I have discovered in my research is that this product is already part of a very well-developed collection of items within one brand, possibly up to 100 or more items. What I have in mind is virtually going head-to-head with this brand using my own brand (more or less duplicating the line). I realize this is thinking way too far forward, but it is hard not to given the apparent success of this competitor. No question I can differentiate from their product line and possibly improve it. Is it a good idea to go up against a competitor like this?** 

You'll only have to compete with them on one product. Don't worry about my main competitor having 60 products to sell; I easily outranked him for the one product I was selling, so don't be concerned. 

-   **Does it matter whether it is yourbrandname.com or yourbrandname.net?** 

The .com is what you really want. 

-   **How important is it for the brand name to go with the product you are selling? I would like to sell products from different categories, so maybe a more generic name is OK?** 

Yes, you may use a more generic name. 

-   **I am selling a product right now on Amazon and there are other sellers that are selling the same product. I found out the supplier doesn't mind me Private Labeling the product. This product that I am selling is very popular and it's not a good idea to change the brand name on it because I will lose a lot of sales this way. What I am looking to do is to create my new unique listing in the way that my competitors won't be able to list under me and in the same way to keep the original brand name. Is it enough to have the supplier use my UPC code instead of the supplier’s UPC code or should I add something to the product name to differentiate it from the original product name (e.g. “product name MX”, where ‘MX’ is my addition)?** 

On Amazon, you can't create a separate listing for an existing product; instead, you must private label it. You should have no trouble listing your private label version and making a lot of sales unless it is a major brand.

-   **I would like to take an existing product and, with some minor changes (e.g. packaging, brand name) that would make it “invisible” to the consumer that my product is NOT the existing one. I want to make sure I don’t break any Amazon rules while at the same time making it so some of the existing consumers of that brand may be willing to purchase my private-­­labeled product over the existing branded one. Is this a sound approach?** 

You really want to label the product as your own. In the long run, it is by far the best option. If you're concerned that you won't be able to compete with the original product, all of the promotional techniques you'll learn in later modules will help you do so. I'm not sure what kind of trademark or patent the product has, but copying the product name might be difficult.

-   **What if my domain is only available with the hyphen xxx-­­xxx.com, is that okay?**

Personally, I don't think that's a good idea. Is it possible to add a word or a character? If your company name is "doghouses.com," for example, try "edoghouses.com." If none of those options are available, you can use the hyphen, but I personally dislike using it.

-   **Other people buy this product and mass sell it. Does the product name just protect me on Amazon?** 

You're missing the point of private labeling, in my opinion. You'll have your own label on it, which means you'll be the only one who can sell it under your name.

-   **Are we using the .com for connection to our Amazon page or are we actually building a site around it?** 

Without a doubt, you'll create a website around it. If you do not have a website for your products and brand, customers will complain and prospects will not buy due to a lack of trust.

-   **By “brand” in this usage, do we mean the “seller name” we set up in Amazon Seller Central? I set that up with a company name (really just a placeholder for now, not my real company name). Or is “brand” separate from “seller”?** 

It is the brand name for your product, such as Apple for the iPhone. 

-   **Should we register our brand name as a trademark?**

If you want to, it's not a major priority when you're FIRST starting though

-   **If we change our brand name or product name or supplier in the future, will that mean a new product listing which then results in losing any reviews we have?** 

You can still use your existing listing.

## **Designing and Packaging your Product**  

-   **Is it advisable or necessary to have our product boxed when selling on Amazon? (My product is molded plastic that can have a label affixed)?** 

If it's fragile, you may want to get a box for it. There is no need to do this, but it is a great strategy for later because you can insert inserts in the boxes (we will discuss this later on). 

-   **Does Amazon allow you to put a web address on the label? I think the answer is no as Amazon wouldn’t want customers bypassing Amazon and buying directly off the seller’s site.** 

All of my labels include a URL. You just don't want it to be in the spotlight, but it is possible to have it with your contact information on the label.

-   **Do we need a design for the actual product and a separate one for the outer box (if it comes in a box) or can both product designs be used for both?** 

It can be used for either, as long as the details are kept the same. 

-   **I have a design. Does Upwork make the labels?** 

The best way to get labels printed is to send your design to the supplier. 

-   **I have no idea where to start with my logo. What do I need to know? What do I need to get done and where?** 

You can hire someone who is professional in making and designing logos at Upwork

-   **I am in Canada and will be selling the product in the US primarily and the source manufacturer is overseas. I am creating a brand and trademark and ultimately a website for my product(s). Do I still need to search for a US trademark?** 

When selling in the US, you need to conduct a US trademark search. 

-   **I sourced my product from overseas. They have the product blister packed on an unbranded BUT VERY APPEALING card -­­ specifying all benefits with images. I can do a “test run” using their packaging (without my logo etc.) and have it at Amazon very soon, or alternatively have to go through the process and have it at Amazon in 4 to 6 weeks. Should I consider option one or NOT AT ALL?** 

It's a super way to get started (sell it under your listing but rebrand ASAP). 

-   **Am I right in assuming that on your label you should have both your Brand Name (i.e. Wonder Products) and Product Name listed (i.e. Super Cool Widget Tool) along with contact details, logo, etc.?** 

This is only for UPC codes, not for labels on the products. 

-   **Which comes first -­­ finding the suppliers and the product first then designing the brand/logo and packaging or vice-­­versa?** 

If you haven't decided which product and supplier to go with, don't worry about the labeling because it will be the same process regardless of which supplier you choose. Prioritize getting your product and supplier selected.

-   **What if I can make up a name for the product, but the manufacturer wants his company name to be on the label instead of me making up a random manufacturer's name?**

If at all possible, avoid putting the manufacturer's name on the product. But if that helps you get started, go for it; your goal is to get a product up and running on Amazon as soon as possible.

-   **The product I have ordered comes in a box complete with images and product descriptions on the box. At this stage, it would not be practical for me to provide my own boxes with my own designs. Also, it may look odd if I just place my own label on the box as it will cover up the original. How can I get around this? Should I just provide plastic bags with my own label on them or ask the supplier to pack in a plain box and attach my label?** 

It's ideal to use custom packaging, otherwise, it won't be a private label product. I would talk with the manufacturer about this.

-   **My supplier sent me the dimensions of the box in order to give to a designer so that the supplier will print the box with the design and logo. What do I ask the designer for? Is it a template? Really not sure where to go from here as my supplier is currently waiting for the design.** 

You can ask your supplier what they need and then send those requirements to your designer.
