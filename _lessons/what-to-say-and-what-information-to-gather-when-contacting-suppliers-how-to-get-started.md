### **Tips for Contacting Suppliers**

1.  **You can contact them via email, but calling is preferable.** 

You will be able to establish a relationship with them as a result of this. If you're working with a supplier from another country, email may be your only option. It's also easier to get information from a supplier if you call them on the phone. It shows you're a real person, and it shows you're serious because you took the extra step of picking up the phone rather than sending a mass email. 

2.  **Having multiple options is ideal.** 

Don't let the fact that you're contacting multiple suppliers overwhelm you. There's only one you'll ever need. If you find a good supplier, feel free to stick with them. Why is it ideal? Because if something does not go as planned, you can easily switch to another supplier.

3.  **Be professional.**

Maintain a professional demeanour. Because you're doing business, show them how you want to be perceived on their end. You may feel awkward if you haven't done this before, but you still want to appear professional. Believe in yourself and what you're learning. These are just people on the other end of the phone, and they rely on you as a supplier.

### **What You Want to Know About the Supplier**   

Since you already know the tips for contacting suppliers. I believe this is the time for you to determine what information you need to know from them.

**1\. Know if they have, or can they get, the product you're looking for.**

You should call this supplier just to make sure they have the type of product you're looking for because you found them while looking for suppliers who meet this product opportunity. If they don't know anyone who does, ask them if they know anyone who does.

**2\. Know if it is possible for them to label your product with your own brand and how the private/custom labelling process works with them.**

First and foremost, we want to sell products under our own label. Working with a company that only produces its own brand and does not private label for others is not worth your time. As a result, as soon as possible, find out if you will be able to use your own packaging with the product.

**3\. Know what are the price tiers for ordering the product they have.** 

They may require you to register as a supplier before providing you with a price sheet, but you still need to know the price tiers for ordering the product as soon as possible. This will indicate whether or not you should pursue this supplier.

\- Discover Minimum Order Quantity (MOQ) 

The MOQ is the smallest amount of inventory you can order from a supplier in order to work with them. You don't want to start by asking what the minimum order quantity is because they might think you're a small business, but it's still useful to know. Even if you're just getting started, you want to give the impression that you're in it for the long haul. You can tell them you're putting them through their paces as a supplier to see if they're up to the task. It's an effective way of keeping them on their toes. Of course, you might not want to put it that way. You want to be professional in your wording, but you also want to have that kind of presence.

**4\. Know the estimated time until the product will be ready to ship.**

Whether you send them your custom label or they do it for you, inquire about the time it will take for the product to be shipped and if there is any way to expedite the process. The time it takes would make sense if you were making a custom product, which could take up to 8 weeks or longer, but you're just putting your label on an existing product that they already have, so it should only take a couple of weeks at most. You can learn how to optimize your Amazon product listing while you wait for your product

**5\. How to get set up to buy?**

And, of course, before you can use their product or service, customers must go through their own process. It's always a good idea to understand their process!

### **How to Get Set Up with a Supplier**

1.  **Ask Them About the Setup Process** 
    

The most likely thing you will need to do is complete some forms. It is standard for most suppliers. 

2.  **Payment Method** 
    

You'll need to decide on a payment method. Most suppliers will not accept a payment method such as PayPal. Credit cards and debit cards are the most common payment methods. You may be able to pay with a check or a wire transfer. You'll have to talk to the supplier about it. You'll also need to pay in advance. Most suppliers won't accept a Net 30 payment when you're getting started.

3.  **Get Product/Packaging Design Specifications**  
    

Some private labelling companies for generic products will design the label for you, and you can tell them what you want on it. However, if they do not design for you, you must obtain the design specifications in order to send it to a designer. Another lesson will go over this. File type, image size, if they have a template (ideally, you want a template) and/or examples of other design files for that product, and any other information you'll need to design packaging labels for that product are the specifications you'll need to get.

4.  **Get Design & Send to Supplier**  
    

Create the design and send it to the supplier in the appropriate format. 

5.  **Send Supplier FBA Shipment Items**  
    

This is covered in more detail in a later lesson, but basically, you're sending the supplier a packing slip for the inside of the box and a shipping label for the outside. This is so that they can ship your items to FBA.
