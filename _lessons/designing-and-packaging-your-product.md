In this lesson, we will discuss how to design good product packaging. It is absolutely essential to your Amazon success. 

### ![Designing and packaging your product](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/Module-3-Image-8-300x185.png)

### **Design Impact**

1.  **Sales** 
    

People will buy your product if it is better designed than all of your competitors, even if it is slightly more expensive, rather than buying someone else's product that is essentially the same as yours under the label.

2.  **Brand Value** 
    

The primary goal of making the first sale is not the only one. When the buyer receives and begins using the product, the excellent design and packaging will leave an impression on them. They will associate your brand with a higher-quality product, prompting them to buy it again, as well as another of your products, and to tell their friends about it. Your product's perceived value must be higher, which can be aided by good packaging and design.

### **How to Design Your Own Label**

1.  **Begin by checking with your supplier.**
    

Talk with your supplier and see if they do that themselves. Suppliers may provide design assistance, sometimes for free. They may or may not be the best designers, but it's worth checking out. 

2.  **Look for a Professional Designer who can assist you.**
    

If you need to hire your own professional designer. Here are some good sites to consider are: 

-   -   [https://www.upwork.com/](https://www.upwork.com/)
        

-   -   [https://www.fiverr.com/](https://www.fiverr.com/) 
        

This will probably be a little more expensive, but it’s a good source as you can tap into the whole crowd-sourcing model. 

### **Key Things to Include on Your Product Label** 

1.  **Your Trademark** 
    

This includes your Brand Name and Logo. While having your design created, you should consider getting a logo created for your brand, not only to make this product look more professional but also for future products under the same brand.

2.  **Product Name**  
    
3.  **Required Information (e.g. warning labels, use, direction and care)**  
    

Examining what your competitors are doing and speaking with your supplier are the best ways to figure out what might be required. While they are not attorneys and cannot give you legal advice, this is the best way to find out what may be required.

4.  **Contact Information**  
    

In some markets, contact information is required, but even if it isn't, it's a good way to provide good customer service to users and potential users of your product in case they have questions or need to contact you for other reasons. A business name, address, phone number, social media accounts, and/or website URL could all be included.

5.  **Benefits of Your Product**  
    

You want to include information such as why people should use your product and how it differs from others because, even if the customer has already purchased the product, you want to persuade them to use it. This is so they will value it and, hopefully, buy another one, tell a friend about it, and buy another of your products.

After you've completed all of this, double-check everything for accuracy because you don't want to print thousands of labels only to discover that one of them is defective. Obtain feedback on your packaging and design as well. Because they're consumers like you and me, you can use family or friends for this. Don't just rely on your own judgment. You should be able to come up with a very high-quality, differentiated, and well-selling design by soliciting feedback from others.
