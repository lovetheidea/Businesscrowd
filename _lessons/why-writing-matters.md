Blogging is a crucial component of generating leads and building a network. It allows those in the early stages of the purchasing journey to engage with you. Considering it can take 15 engagements before a purchase, blogging can act as the first or several engagements, helping to move a new contact into a position when they’re ready to buy.

But there are numerous ways that blogging helps. Here are some of the key aspects you’d benefit from.

### 1\. Improve Your Credentials

Your credentials, or perceived value to the prospective client, are essential. You want to show why you’re better than other potential developers.

Blogging is known to improve several credentials, such as:

-   Showcase current skills and work ethics.
-   Demonstrate how you problem-solve.
-   Prove the hourly rate/project rate that you charge.

And B2B buyers, the primary source of your income, read blogs. More than 7 out of 10 read blogs during their purchasing journey to research the supplier/talent and to see if they have aligned ethical beliefs.

As your credentials improve, so does the potential for your hourly rate.

### 2\. Adds Inbound Marketing

Blogging adds a new marketing stream called inbound marketing, where leads are generated passively from those seeking knowledge/services. These leads can be contacted through follow-up marketing channels such as email marketing.

Research has shown that lead generation can be 80% higher for those who blog about 15 times monthly. Website traffic can also increase by 55%, partly thanks to a 434% increase in website ranking potential. And you can get a 1300% ROI on blogging.

It can also make further marketing much more lucrative. SEO-generated leads have a close rate of 14.6%, compared with just 1.7% of outbound marketing techniques.

### 3\. Add to Your Soft Skills Set

Blogging can help with your soft skills, which can aid in the delivery of proposals and projects. Writing skills, communications, networking, and project management can improve with regular blog writing.

Soft skills are essential to success at work, whether you’re a freelancer or an employee.
