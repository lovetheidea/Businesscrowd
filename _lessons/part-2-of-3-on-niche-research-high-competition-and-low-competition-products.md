## **The Big Question: High Competition vs. Low Competition** **Products** 

The big question many people have is: 

**"Which is more profitable: being one of many sellers in a crowded market or being one of only a few sellers in a less crowded market?"**

What is the solution? It depends, but a crowded market is preferable for physical products, especially for new entrepreneurs.

This may appear to be a little counterintuitive. You're probably thinking that choosing a crowded market when you're just starting out makes more sense for someone who has been there longer and is ready to take on the higher competition.

But that is not the case here.  

**First,** the upside potential in that crowded market is greater. You can do well as a middle-of-the-road seller, but once you start ramping things up, there's a lot more upside potential than there is for low-competition products.

**Second,** a crowded market allows you to focus more effectively. You don't need a slew of different products in a market with high competition and high upside potential. You can have one product that completely blows everything else out of the water for you, meeting every financial goal you could possibly have. With limited resources, this allows you to focus more effectively. You don't need a large staff to run the show.

## **What Not to Pick** 

In a word, avoid picking the Long Tail.

If you're unfamiliar with the phrase, it was popularized by a man called Chris Anderson, who was discussing how much money individuals were earning on the long-tail, whether it was via SEO targeting long-tail keywords or selling long-tail goods on Amazon due to its digital distribution. It's the ability to sell one or two units of a million distinct items on an irregular basis. He was implying that this is a new wave, that this is an excellent business strategy, and so on.

## **Not for this course** 

Believe me when I say that the most successful individuals I know who sell tangible goods have a very restricted product portfolio. Generally, one is the optimal number, perhaps two or even five, but that is the most effective method to completely dominate. Take care not to pluck the long tail. Choose a competitive sport.

## **Why Not Pick the Long-tail?** 

There are various reasons why you should avoid long-tail keywords:

-   Products with little competition have restricted upside potential.
-   Multiple items are required to earn the same amount of money. You may require five low-competition goods to earn the same amount of money as one high-competition product, but with more effort.
-   You must handle a variety of items and inventories. This may become a little annoying. It's considerably simpler to maintain track of a single product.
-   You lose the reduced cost advantage of buying 1 product in bulk. If you’re splitting your focus between several different products, this means you’re not focusing enough on 1 individual product. If you put all your focus and effort into 1 individual product, you’re going to start ramping it up and the more volume you better prices you’ll get. So, that means the more profit you’ll make for every single sale. It’s a huge advantage of focusing on 1 product.

For example, I know a guy who has been selling 1 product for the past five years or so. He currently sells it for $40 and buys it for $2 because he keeps moving up the manufacturing chain. Now that is a good profit margin. That’s what we want you to start aiming for. You may never hit it. I don’t have products that have that kind of profit margin, but that’s something you should be aiming for: 500, 600, even 700 per cent profit. That’s what we want you to achieve. 

-   There’s an additional financial outlay. If you have to sell multiple products, test multiple products, and buy more inventory, there’s an additional financial outlay. ∙ When you don’t pick the long-tail there’s no need to “hedge your bets”. We will help you pick winners. A lot of people think they have to try a lot of different products because they don’t know which one will do well. Trust me, in Module 2 we’ll teach you how to pick winning products every single time. Some may do better than others, but we’ll teach you how to pick winning products. There’s really no need to hedge your bets and it actually doesn’t make any sense in this business model. 

## **Choose High Volume Products** 

You should still look for a **healthy competitive landscape**. It doesn't mean you should go after products that are too difficult to compete with. It does not necessarily mean whether or not the product has a large number of reviews versus another opportunity that is selling just as well but does not have that kind of thing going on. You obviously want to go with the one that is selling just as well but is a newer product and may not be as competitive as the other one.

You must still use common sense here, but choose products that are already selling extremely well. Don't try to be the first to market. Don't bother pursuing long-tail products. Don't try to come up with something new. Choose a product that is already selling extremely well.  

More detail will be covered in the next module on picking product opportunities. We’ll dive very, very deep into picking product opportunities. We’ll teach you more about how to pick good, high-competition products, and then you can still jump in there and absolutely dominate.  

This is just giving you an overview. Don’t think you’re going to have to go out there and manage  52 products or that it’s even a good idea. The holy grail of this business is having 1 product that absolutely dominates, where you are the market leader, where you actually crush it with that product.  

It may or may not happen. You may end up having 2 or 3 or 5 products, but having one product that dominates is the ideal situation with this business model.
