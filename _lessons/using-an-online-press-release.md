## **What is an Online Press Release?** 

An **Online Press Release** is a popular way to inform the media about your business and its offerings. Because these press releases are published on a variety of websites (press release directories, Google News, Yahoo News, and others) that then link directly to what you're promoting, and many of these websites can send a decent amount of targeted traffic, it's also a low-cost and highly effective method of driving traffic to specific web pages.

## **Benefits of Online Press Releases For Your Product Launch**

-   **Excellent tool for public relations and marketing.** 
    

Hundreds of millions of people use Google News and Yahoo News each month to get the latest news on a variety of topics that interest them. They frequently find relevant press releases as well as articles from thousands of news sources when conducting a news search.

A properly used press release can drive large amounts of traffic to any website and create backlinks that will last for a long time, regardless of what business you are in.

**Please take note:** Press releases are a powerful marketing tool that should be used only when you have something new to announce. Because the primary purpose of a press release is to inform the world about your news item, don't use it as a hyped-up advertisement to make a sale, or the press release directories will reject it.

-   **There is always a need for them.** 
    

Press releases are used by all news organizations, including magazine editors, broadcast editors, and industry-specific editors, to develop the majority of their published news stories.

Editors who report on your news release are considered disinterested parties from the perspective of consumers, implying that your announcement was chosen due to public demand for relevant and useful information. Paid advertising is frequently viewed as suspect by customers because companies are more concerned with the sale of their products than with the best interests of their customers.

-   **Boost the Credibility of your Brand.**
    

Consider being featured by those reputable journalists or any other media outlet. It will not only broaden your brand's audience but also boost its credibility.

## **What Should You Write About?** 

Press releases are extremely popular, and thousands of people submit them every day in the hopes of being noticed. The issue is that a press release should not be used whenever you simply want to send out some general information. Rather, you should always ensure that they have a newsworthy angle. But don't get caught up in the word "newsworthy"; it's not as difficult as you might think

### **Improve Your SEO from Press Releases**

The majority of websites that publish submitted press releases allow for links, press releases can be very helpful in improving search engine rankings for the pages that are linked.

There's a reason why everything seems to revolve around search engine optimization. SEO now extends far beyond keywords, influencing everything from your website's image descriptions and alt tags to its content, links, and citations. Although it is uncommon, a press release can directly improve your company's search engine results pages (SERPs) if its URL ranks.

**Rules of Getting SEO Benefit from Press Releases** 

1.  1.  **Create high-quality content:** It was common practice to use low-quality writers to churn out press releases, which resulted in a complete garbage piece. Press releases are most effective when the organization has newsworthy pieces that are groundbreaking, innovative, interesting, and valuable to readers, so make sure to consider that piece. Don't forget to include an interesting headline or you'll miss it.

1.  2.  **Do not use the same anchor text for multiple hyperlinks:** The next most common error made by press release publishers is to hyperlink the same keyword every time they release a new press release. This is referred to as anchor text, and it will appear in HTML as a href="http://yourwebpage.com">Anchor Text Here/a>. Although it is well known that the anchor text of a link influences what the page being linked to can rank for in the search engines, Google (and some of the other search engines) have made some changes as a result of widespread abuse. They primarily look for different press releases that all use the same anchor text, and once found, they simply ignore those links. This does not mean you will be penalized if you submit one press release to multiple press release sites; rather, you will be penalized if you submit multiple press releases repeatedly, all of which use the same anchor text and link to the same URL (web page). We recommend that you change the anchor text on every press release you send out. 

1.  3.  **Do not link to the same URL every time**: Another major blunder many people make when submitting a press release is linking to the same URL every time. If you're using Amazon as an example, make sure you're not just linking to your product page every time, but also include links to other Amazon pages that then link directly to your product page. Reviews and your seller page (the page that appears when they click the "sold by" link on your product listing) are two examples of this. This isn't to say you shouldn't continue to promote your product listing page, but make sure it's not the only one you're linking to and that you're not linking to it with every submission (rotate it up).

1.  4.  **Use press releases to promote other promotions**: This rule complements the previous rule by ensuring that all of your press releases do not use the same URL. It's EXTREMELY effective if you also promote other promotions you've run. If you create a new web page, upload a new YouTube video, or someone writes a blog post about your product, send out a press release to let everyone know. You can include a few different links in your press release submission, and I always recommend that at least one of the links in the content points to a promotion. This could be anything from a video to a tweet on Twitter or even a press release.

1.  5.  **Distribute to the Right People**: You are, however, attempting to build a business here, which means you will need to do a lot of promotion. You may consider sending as many press releases as possible without doing so properly. But what is the correct method? Distribute the piece to the most appropriate sources, including those who are a good fit for your location and audience, as well as those who are most likely to cover the story. Expecting spectacular results from a single or even a couple of press releases is unrealistic. This is an excellent job to outsource and have someone write a new press release for you.

**TIP**: Use Google Alerts to be notified when new content about your company or products is published online. This way, if you're alerted to a webpage that's giving your product a good review, you can promote it and help it drive more traffic to your site. If, on the other hand, the webpage is negative, you can contact them to try to resolve the problem.

## **How to Write a Press Release?** 

While distributing a press release is a popular way to obtain backlinks to your website, you must ensure that you are not solely interested in obtaining links. It's critical to concentrate on getting people to read your press release and visit your website.

A good press release answers the questions of who, what, where, when, and why you're sharing information about your company, product, service, or event. Rewrite your press release if it reads like an advertisement.

The best way to get started is to pick an angle that will catch the reader's eye. You'll need to think outside the box on this one. The most effective methods to use are:

1.  1.  **Humanize Content:** Everyone admires a hero, and they enjoy hearing about success (and failure), so tell them about it. Relate your story in such a way that it elicits a strong emotional reaction from your audience.  Launch Press Releases are good, but a better one comes later when true success (or failure) can be discussed.
    2.  **Take a Stand:** Every day, thousands of new media stories are published. The majority of them go unnoticed, but a select few stand out so take a stand on an issue and connect your story to it.
    3.  **Focus on the Time of Year:** summer, winter, Christmas, July 4th, or whatever the occasion, relate to it somehow.
    4.  **Be a Trend Communicator:** New trends emerge regularly. Discover what is becoming increasingly popular and relate to it.

After you've determined your "Angle," begin writing down your thoughts (or screen). Remember the **A-I-D-A** method we talked about when writing a blog post? You can also use it to write your press release. Keep watching this video to learn how to use this formula when writing a press release.

Let's start with:

**Attention:** The most important aspect of any writing is... You need to get the reader's attention, whether it's for news or sales, and your press release headline is responsible for this. You only have a few seconds to catch your readers' attention. Don't blow it with a shaky start.

**Interest:** Okay, you've got your reader's attention... So, what now? You must pique your reader's interest in what you are writing about in order for them to continue reading. This is the job of the first (summary) paragraph of your press release, and this is where you really need to connect with the reader's emotional side. Do not simply repeat the headline; instead, relate to the subject. Simply do it in a different way.

**Desire:** Once you've piqued the reader's interest, you need to pique their desire to take the next step (Action). You must exercise extreme caution in order to avoid making this a sales letter, but you still want your reader to take action and visit your product or website. The only way to accomplish this is to pique their interest in learning more about you. This is why an angle is so crucial.

 You don't have a lot of words to work within a press release, and you need to stay very focused on your subject, so make sure you:

-   -   -   **Use Action Words** \- Verbs in the active voice breathe life into your press release.
        -   **Watch Your Word Count** \- Only use as many words as you need to tell your story. Do it if you can tell your story in fewer words. The use of unnecessary words detracts from your story. Keep it short and sweet. Make every word matter.
        -   **Optimize for Search Engines** \- Make sure the press release includes the keywords you want to rank for. Don't stuff your content with keywords; instead, make sure it reads well. It's best to focus on one main keyword and include it once in the title and three or four times in the body.
        -   **Action** – Okay, now that you've piqued their interest, you need to tell them how to follow through on their desire. Give them your name, email/autoresponder, website, phone number, and other pertinent information. Give them every opportunity to obtain additional information. Give more than one option!
        -   **Stick to the Facts** \- Tell it like it is. Exaggerations, embellishments, and fluff should all be avoided. If you believe your press release is embellished, you should reconsider it. If your story sounds too good to be true, you’re likely jeopardizing your credibility. Even if it's accurate, you might want to tone it down a little.
        -   **Avoid the hype** \- The exclamation mark (!) is your adversary. There is no other way to put it. It is better to destroy your credibility than to include a lot of hype. If an exclamation point is required, use one. Never, ever do this!!!!!!!!!!!!!
        -   **Be Unique** \- Your press release must be one-of-a-kind.

## **Formatting Your Press Release** 

-   **300 to 600 Words** \- Keep your press release short and simple. 300 to 600 words are best. 
-   **Mixed case** \- A PRESS RELEASE SHOULD NEVER BE SUBMITTED IN ALL CASE LETTERS. This is a dreadful example of proper etiquette. Even if your press release gets past the editors - which is unlikely but not impossible - journalists will almost certainly dismiss it. A mixed case is a good option.
-   **Correct grammar usage** \- Always adhere to grammatical and stylistic conventions. Your credibility is harmed by grammatical and stylistic errors. Your press release will be rejected if it contains too many errors. 
-   **Word processor** \- Instead of composing your press release online, write it in a word processor. Online writing will not yield the best results. Take your time and do it correctly. Write, print, and proofread your work. Rewrite and revise.
-   **No HTML** \- In your press release, never use HTML or other markup languages. Your press release will be distributed through a variety of channels. The readability of your press release will suffer as a result of such formatting.
-   **More than one paragraph** \-Telling your story in a few sentences is nearly impossible. If you don't have more than a few sentences, you probably don't have anything newsworthy.
-   **Headline -** The headline is the first step in the writing process. The headline is a one-sentence statement that summarizes the story. Companies, organizations, and individuals have a plethora of options for publicizing their news. A new product or service, a new feature, a new website, a new office location, a new employee, or a new award are all examples of company news. In 25 words or less, the lead sentence conveys the most important information. Here's your chance to grab the reader's attention. Also, don't assume your reader has read your headline or summary paragraph; the lead should be able to stand alone.
-   **Body of Press Release -** The "Who, What, When, Where, How, and Why" of the announcement or news are covered in the body of the press release. The "facts" about the announcement or news should be the only details in a press release. It is not appropriate to use opinions, fluff, or hype. Statements like "this is the most amazing invention ever invented" should be avoided. Readers must form their own opinions based on the press release's details (facts). 
-   **Do Not Say “I”** \- Keep your press release's goal in mind at all times. Except in direct quotes, do not use the words 'I,' 'we,' or 'you.'

## **Common Press Release Mistakes** 

Some of the most common errors in press releases can be found in the list below. These are not the same as the SEO Rules I listed above; instead, they focus on errors that prevent your submission from being published or picked up by media sites (and even Google News). A poorly written release does not give you a second chance to correct the negative impressions it leaves.

-   -   -   **All Upper Case Characters** – Never send a press release in all capital letters. It's been said before, but it bears repeating. The headline and body of your press release should be in the proper case.
        -   **Grammatical Errors** \- Even the best writers are prone to making grammatical and typographical errors. Your press release should be proofread, edited, and reproofed. 
        -   **Lack of Content** \- Approximately 10% of all press release submissions is turned down due to a lack of content. To ensure a complete press release, please answer all of the "W" questions: who, what, where, when, why, and how.
        -   **Press Releases that Scream BUY ME! -** Do not write your press release as if it were a commercial. Journalists are not your marketing partners, so keep that in mind. It is not their job to sell, but to relay information to their audience. A good press release keeps the media informed. If your press release screams "BUY ME," you might want to rewrite it. 
        -   **Hype Flags** \- This problem is related to the "BUY ME" issue. If your press release has too many "hype flags," spam filters will catch it and intercept it before it reaches its intended recipient. Anything that calls your press release's credibility into question is referred to as a "Hype Flag." Exclamation points aplenty or outrageous product and service claims are examples of "Hype Flags."
        -   **Funny Characters** \- During the submission (copy and paste) process, strange characters and formatting may appear in your press release. Make certain that your press release is formatted correctly.
        -   **Word Wrapping** \- Each line should not be broken at 70 characters. Allow your sentences to naturally wrap around each other. At the end of each line, do not use a hard carriage return. At the end of each paragraph, use a carriage return only.
        -   **Incorrect Usage of E-mail** \- This is a problem that affects about 30% of all press releases. Instead of a "personal" account, use a "role" account. Pr@companyname.com is a role account, where 'pr' stands for "public relations." Pat@companyname.com, whose personal name is Pat, is an example of a personal account.

-   -   -   **Using a Call To Action** \- No call to action text should be included in any part of your press release. This means you shouldn't say things like "go to learn more," "check them out at," "click this link," or anything else along those lines. This screams marketing, and as previously stated, press releases are for news, not advertising, and most press release distribution websites will reject your submission if it includes a call to action.

That doesn't mean you have to write the press releases yourself; however, if you don't know what you're doing, you won't want to delegate the task to someone else and miss out on the process. Setting a goal for yourself this week to write and submit seven press releases in the next seven days is a good idea. After seven days, you'll be well on your way to promoting your product; you'll have a good understanding of how the system works and what the outsourcer will be expected to do.

Below is an action plan based on the assumption that your product is in stock on Amazon. 

**Day 1:** Launch of the new product: The purpose of this press release is to announce the release of your new product. Other than a link to the product on Amazon, this release is focused on the product and makes no mention of Amazon (this will be covered in the next release).

**Day 2:** The purpose of this press release is to inform you that your product is now available on Amazon. Even though you've already announced a new product, this one is solely focused on your Amazon presence.

**Day 3:** Promotional launch of a new product with a limited-time special offer that expires on a certain date: Setting a sale price for your product and assigning an end date will be required for this release.

**Day 4:** Announcement of Customer Experience with Product: This release focuses on the first reviews you have received for your product. In the press release, link directly to the positive ones and quote sections of the n reviews.

**Day 5:** Announcement of free shipping**:** Amazon is offering free shipping if the order includes at least $25 of eligible items. This release mention how they can get free shipping if they order today.

**Day 6:** Announce the 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee: Amazon offers a 30-Day Money-Back Guarantee, which you can promote in a press release. In the press release, you should also include a link to a webpage with more information about the guarantee.

**Day 7:** End of Sale Price Announcement: This press release simply announces the sale's end. This is a future date that should be set (a few days or longer).

**NOTE:** Instead of promoting a sale price, announce the creation of various social accounts, such as a product/brand-specific YouTube channel, Facebook page, and/or Twitter account, if your product isn't yet available. As you can see from the above 7 examples and the other topic ideas, there are a plethora of topics on which you can write. Make no excuses and get down to business.

## **Working Backward Approach By Amazon**

At Amazon, a method known as "working backwards" is widely used. On Quora a few years ago, Ian McAllister, Amazon's former Director of Delivery Experience and Alexa International, provided an insightful look into Amazon's approach to product development. Working backwards, according to McAllister, starts with "trying to work backwards from the customer, rather than starting with an idea for a product and trying to bolt customers onto it."

According to McAllister, the product manager will typically start a new initiative by writing an internal press release announcing the finished product. This press release's target audience is the product's customer, and it focuses on the customer problem, how existing solutions fail, and how the new product will outperform existing solutions.

**How this method can help you?** 

-   -   -   It's a good way to see if your new product is worthwhile to produce. When a press release is successful and customers become excited and interested in it, it simply means that it is worth pursuing.
        -   It can also be used as a guide during the development process of a product. And, as you develop your product, this press release will serve as a guide to see if you are still on track with what you stated in it.
        -   Less expensive to make, which is a wise decision.
