### **Google Search Hack**

Keep in mind that suppliers are notoriously bad at marketing as you go through this. As a result, finding a supplier for your product on the first page of a Google search might not yield the desired results. You may already have the habit of not looking past the first page of Google results when searching for something. This means you'll have to dig a little deeper, perhaps two, three, or even four pages deep.

But don't be worried. This lesson will show you how to find suppliers for almost any product you find on Amazon in the simplest way possible. Here are some great ideas to consider as you look for a supplier:

-   Google gives you access to 99% of suppliers. 
-   There is no need to search through outdated supplier directories. Believe me, I've wasted plenty of time searching obsolete supplier directories and attending trade shows. Google is, by far, the easiest and fastest way to find suppliers for almost any product. 

-   You'll save TONS of time by learning a few Google search tricks. Most of us, as consumers, type a keyword into Google and see what comes up. If we don't like what pops up, we modify our searches to refine our results based on different keywords. If you are not familiar with it, there are a few tricks you can use to find suppliers on Google. 

# **Google Search Tricks to Find Suppliers** 

There are a few tricks you can use that will assist you in your search for suppliers using Google:

**1\. Search for Exact Phrases** 

-   Put quotation marks around **“keywords”**. 
-   This will pull up websites that contain that EXACT phrase. If you put in a generic keyword with no quotes, especially if it’s multiple words (e.g. a keyword phrase), the results will be all over the place.
-   A search for _"blue cat brush"_ will miss pages that refer to blue cats brushes. This way, if you are specifically looking for a blue cat brush this will allow you to search for suppliers a lot faster and more effectively. 

First, Google will show you the images of the Blue cat brush.

![Images of the Blue cat brush](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/images-of-the-Blue-cat-brush-300x244.png)

Next to it is the list of stores or any links that will direct you to a blue cat brush.

![List of stores](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/list-of-stores-300x231.png)

**2\. Use the 3 Main Supplier Search Phrases** 

As shown in the following three phrases, you can use them to help you in your search:  

**• \[product keyword\] private label** (i.e. _blue cat brush private label or "blue cat brush" private label_) 

This isn't just going to find you blue widgets selling retail; it's also going to find you, private label suppliers.

![Private Label](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Private-Label-300x224.png)

**• \[product keyword\] supplier** (i.e. _blue cat brush supplier or “blue cat brush” supplier_)  

![Product keyword supplier](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/product-keyword-supplier-300x262.png)

**• \[product keyword\] wholesale** (i.e. _blue cat brush wholesale or “blue cat brush” wholesale_) 

**\*Tip:** Try putting the product keyword in quotation marks: **“product keyword”**. As mentioned, suppliers are not extremely good at marketing themselves so that they can be found via Google, so you will want to play around with this to see what pops up. Remember that Google searches for an exact phrase when you put the product keyword in quotation marks, such as "blue widget" private label. 

**3\. Exclude Sites** 

•Use **site:website.com “product keyword”** to exclude a site from search results. Here are some examples: 

• Example Search: site:facebook.com “blue cat brush” 

This will pull up all the blue cat brush supplier listings excluding any from facebook.com. You always want to use this one when searching because you get some weird Facebook listings sometimes. Not only that, but you're unlikely to find any suppliers on Facebook.

## **Demonstration of how to do it**

In order for you to fully understand all of this in the context of what you've already accomplished, you can follow along with your own product as I walk you through this. To start: 

-   -   Open your product opportunity spreadsheet from Module 2. You should have 15-20 product opportunities, with your 3 top ones highlighted. 
    -   Pick one of your 3 top opportunities. 
    -   Follow as I search for a supplier using the Google Search Method. 

**Cat Brush** 

Here is the opportunity I have chosen for this walk-through: the _Cat Brush._ The steps I use to find suppliers for this product are as follows: 

1.  1.   Open the URL for the product in a new tab. 
    2.   Open another tab and go to google.com. 

We’re going to start searching using our main keyword; in this case, ‘_Cat Brush_’ and then add a private label, e.g. cat brush private label 

The following are some of the results of the search. Each one is listed here as it appears in the list (including spelling errors and highlights), arranged first by the website they are located at:

Now, we could just start digging through the pages, but we’d like to be as efficient as possible. 

Whenever you find something that might be a good fit, go to your spreadsheet/document or whatever you are using and add a new section titled with the product name. I use a spreadsheet for this, so we create a new sheet and rename it "Potential Product Supplier."

![Potential Product Supplier](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Potential-Product-Supplier-300x91.png)

In this case, we create the template like this format: The first column will be the Product Name, the next column is the Potential Supplier Name, and the next column will be the Supplier website.

![Supplier website](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/Supplier-website-300x30.png)

If you are not finding very good results as you look through the search results list. Replace "private label" with "supplier" or "wholesale" whenever this happens to you. If that doesn't work, consider where else you might be able to find this product. You could inquire, for example:

-   -   How else would a supplier of a generic version of this product be selling it?  
    -   How would they present this product?  

You'll want to get about 5 potential suppliers for each of your three products to make step 4 of the Opportunity Finder System work well for you because you want to have plenty of options. Keep your information well organized (e.g., a separate worksheet for each product) so you can easily keep track of everything you collect.

You also don't need to be concerned about how you'll evaluate these suppliers because that will be covered in later lessons. You'll learn how to contact these suppliers, and you'll have this lesson to refer to if you need to find more.
