As well as completing regular email marketing campaigns, you need to determine how successful those campaigns are. Monitoring campaigns can be a critical way to spot trends in performance that allow you to make changes to various elements that can improve results in the long term.

Many different aspects need to be measured, and numerous ways we can measure these aspects. Therefore, there is a lot to learn about how to and what to measure.

## Key Metrics to Measure in Email Marketing

Numerous statistics email marketing campaigners need to look out for. These include open rates, click-through rates, deliverability, and unsubscribe rates. These basic statistics can provide a quick health check on email success.

**Deliverability**: This is the first statistic that you should look at. Deliverability is a statistic that demonstrates how many emails are reaching the contacts' inboxes. Ideally, you want to aim for 100%, but there are times when deliverability can be lower, for instance, when:

-   There is a soft bounce (the inbox is temporarily unavailable)
-   There is a hard bounce (the inbox is permanently unavailable)
-   Spam filters have prevented email from being delivered.

A drop in deliverability doesn’t usually happen unless you’ve bought a mailing list, but there are cases when email addresses no longer work. The average email address only works for about six months.

**Open Rates:** Open rates are the number of people who've clicked to read the email. Calculate this statistic by dividing the number of people who opened the message by the number of sent emails. This statistic does not look at how many times someone might have read the email.

**Click-Through Rates**: This is the number of people sent an email who then clicked on a button or link inside the email. The calculation is done by taking the number of people who clicked and dividing by the mailing list. It does not use the number of people who opened the email as part of the equation, nor does it use multiple clicks from one individual.

**Unsubscribe**: The number of people who have unsubscribed from a mailing list. Calculate this statistic by dividing the number of unsubscribers by the mailing list total. Typically, this is very low.

## What Are The Average Rates for the Software Industry?

Every industry has its benchmark statistics. There are standard rates for all industries. These benchmarks can be critical for determining how your campaigns are performing against the standard.

-   Open Rate: 22.7% (5.5% higher than all sectors)
-   Click-Through: 2.0% (13% lower than all sectors)
-   Unsubscribe Rate: 0.2% (100% higher than all sectors)

These statistics also provide us with an insight into the software industry audience. For instance, we can tell:

-   The audience is highly engaged, being more favourable to open and read emails from software providers.
-   Those developing emails in this industry don't create a compelling offer.
-   You will have to work twice as hard to grow a marketing list as double the numbers will unsubscribe.
-   Audiences prefer to read the content of the emails than act upon the email.

These statistics can help you to develop long-term strategies to help you build better marketing lists and campaigns.

Monitoring Tools

A host of monitoring tools that can help keep track of email marketing performance. These tools can also include sending and automation elements that can help manage the marketing performance of campaigns and offer ways to reduce workloads.

These monitoring tools are:

-   MailChimp
-   Hubspot
-   ActiveCampaign
-   MailerLite
-   Constant Contact
-   Mailjet

You can get some free accounts, which allow you to monitor the success of your website. For example, MailChimp allows you a contact list of 2000 contacts and 10,000 monthly emails. But you won’t get automation tools.

## Measuring Email Marketing Success

Measuring the success of your email marketing campaigns is key. It lets you know how emails are performing and whether or not you need to make changes. For example, seeing campaigns with lower click-through rates can be a clear sign to change call-to-actions. And if you’re suffering from a low open rate, you need to change the open rate.
