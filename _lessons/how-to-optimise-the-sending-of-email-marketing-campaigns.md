In the previous lesson, we’ve spoken about the importance of optimising the content for the reader. 

However, another aspect of ensuring the best performance is to optimise when we’re sending emails and how we’re sending emails for marketing purposes. There can be a drastic difference in performance from sending emails at specific times.

So in this lesson, we’ll look at how we can optimise the sending of email marketing campaigns.

## General Statistics for Sending Emails

There is a lot of data that has been collected about when to send emails. These are generally considered as below:

-   Tuesday is the best day to send emails to corporate clients.
-   Sunday is the worst day to send emails to any audience.
-   The best times to send emails are (in order of preference)
    -   9 am to 12 noon
    -   12 noon to 3 pm
    -   6 am to 9 am
-   Sending emails between 9 pm and midnight is half as effective as sending emails between midnight and 6 am.
-   Marketers report different frequencies of sending emails.
    -   12% send emails weekly
    -   10% send emails twice a week
    -   17% of marketers send emails 3-4 times a week
    -   21% of marketers send emails every day.
    -   22% of businesses and leaders send emails at least 2-3 times a day.
-   Some of these statistics don’t include segregation or drip feeds, which can skew these results.

These statistics can be pretty important because we can use them to help build more effective email marketing campaigns.

## Tactics For Improving Email Marketing Statistics

And we can use some other optimisation tactics to help improve campaigns. So here are some tactics that improve email campaigns.

### **Personalisation**

Personalisation is making a change in an email that is adaptive. It changes based on a key component of the contact, for instance, the first name, last name, last order, etc. Personalisation can be applied to numerous aspects of the email, including the subject line, call-to-action and the body of text.

The basic personalisation of using the first name is a great start, but it is limiting and doesn’t have the same effect as other personalisation options, such as mentioning a previous purchase or conversation.

But any personalisation is better than none and can help with open rates and click-through rates.

### **Segmentation**

Segmentation is when you split a marketing list based on tags that you’ve created. You can improve open rates by 30% and click-through rates by 55%. Segmentation is about sending different email copies to different audiences based on those tags. So you might send past clients something different to those who haven’t ordered yet.

Tags can be based on anything, from position in the company, past relationships, past interactions, location, products they’ve ordered, etc.

### **Testing**

There are various ways that you can test emails. For instance, you can do A/B testing, QA testing and spam testing. A/B testing is when the two emails are sent to a split audience, with one slight difference to see what audiences prefer. One half gets one version, and the other half gets the other version.

Some of the changes that might be tested include:

-   Location of the call-to-action
-   The wording of the call-to-action
-   Video vs image
-   Time of day the email is sent

The idea is that you are testing to see what gets the best results. If you notice that the open rate is better when sent at midday compared to 9 am, then you know that your audience prefers a midday email. You can use this in future campaigns.

### **Automated Emails**

About 50% of all email marketing is automated. Automated emails sometimes include drip feed campaigns or transactional emails (follow-up to sales, birthday emails, etc.). Research has shown that 31% of all email orders come from automated emails. Therefore, they don’t offer their fair share (they should offer 50% of sales) but they still make up a fair proportion of sales.

Automation can be applied in various ways and can be a good way to reduce workloads when busy. 91% of marketers say that email automation helps them to achieve goals.

Perhaps create a drip feed, where you showcase past work and demonstrate how you can help. After 3-4 emails, you can then drop them a message asking if they would like a quote for similar work.

## How to Optimise the Sending of Email Marketing Campaigns

As much as it is about optimising the subject line and content of an email marketing campaign, it is also important to consider how you can optimise the delivery of the campaign. Consider the time of day, the day and other aspects to help you optimise your email marketing. Automation can be a big help, and so can using tests like A/B and Quality Assurance tests.
