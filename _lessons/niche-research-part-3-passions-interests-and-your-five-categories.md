## **Choosing a Product to Sell** 

There are three ways to choose a product to sell: 

1.  **Finding a great opportunity product** - We'll show you exactly how to find products on Amazon that you can private label, put your own label on, create your own brand, and use for the rest of your life and make a lot of money doing so.
2.  **Sell something you already have access to -** This is how a lot of people get started in eCommerce. Chose that type of product, which is not common, but you have access to them.
3.  **Sell your passion, hobbies, or something that you love** \- Consider what you're interested in, such as a hobby or a topic, and go sell something. Since you know a lot of things about it you will now use it to your advantage in the market. 

## **Why Selling Something You Use Is a Good Idea** 

-   **You will know the** **_differentiators_** **that matter to consumers** \- We'll help you in selecting any good selling product you find and doing exceptionally well with it, but you'll have an advantage if it's something you're interested in. If you notice two possibilities, one that is related to something you're interested in and one that you couldn't care less about or is completely irrelevant to you, sell the one that is related to something you're interested in because you'll have some advantages.

The first advantage is that you will see the differentiators that are important to consumers. One of the most important things you'll learn in Module 4 is how to add differentiators to your product. Differentiators are the features that distinguish your goods. Whether it's the design, the title, the description, anything else you include with the product, or something else, it's the little details that can make all the difference in the world. If you're already in the market, you'll be aware of what those are.

-   **You can create images/titles/descriptions that resonate with the audience** \- If you are part of the audience, for example, by using the product yourself, then you are aware of what appeals to that audience and what that audience is looking for. 
-   **You will be more likely to continually improve the product** \- If it's something you use, you'll think of and find ways to make it a little bit better, a little bit easier, to make the instructions clearer, to make the suggestions a little more useful, and so on. You will be more likely to continuously enhance the product and get better and better, as opposed to the competition, who is kind of stagnating (because that's what people do).
-   **You’ll also likely have friends that also use the product themselves** – Which means you'll have an easy time finding reviews. If they use a product similar to the one you want to sell, you can give them your product for free in exchange for a review. If it's something they're interested in, it's a relatively easy sell. If it's something they don't like, they're just being polite.

## **Brainstorming Exercise** 

Before we wrap up this module, let's do some handy brainstorming exercises to get you ready for the next Module.

Module 2 focuses on identifying a good product opportunity. As previously stated, it can be any product that is selling well and satisfies all of the requirements established in Module 2, but if possible, try to make it overlap with yourself, meaning that it is something you are interested in and possibly already use.

Here's a brainstorming exercise to get you ready for Module 2. Make a note of your answers to the following questions on a piece of paper or a notepad where you can easily refer back to them when you need them:

**1) What did you purchase in the last 30 days?**

Don't include things like food and other necessities. Consider any physical thing that you might be able to sell. Take a look around your office. Take a look around your house. Examine your credit card statement. What you've purchased in the last 30 days is a decent predictor of what you're actually utilizing.

**2) What are your passions and interests?** **Do you have any interests or Hobbies?**

An example, (although not necessarily a good private label example) is the GoPro camera. It’s the camera you can stick on yourself, a hat, or anything, to go do some crazy sports like snowboarding, mountain biking, etc. It’s a massively popular thing and a billion-dollar business now, all invented by a guy who had a passion for that kind of product. Now, we’re not inventing stuff here, but that doesn’t mean you can’t get the same advantage from tapping into a market that you’re already interested in.

**3) Select five broad categories from the list you created from the previous two questions.**

It is health, cuisine, decorating, technology, or anything else. Choosing these five major categories will provide you with excellent preparation for Module 2.
