## Potential pitfalls of RSS feeds

RSS feeds are nearly as old as the internet, being an old way that people could find content they wanted. And with their established presence, you would think that legal aspects would be completely sorted. However, there are many ways in which RSS feeds can get you into trouble.

In this lesson, we look at the potential pitfalls of RSS feeds and what can be done about it.

## Why are RSS Feeds Problematic?

There are a couple of reasons why RSS feeds are problematic. For one, they’re using content that can sometimes be out of our control. It takes just a rogue publisher or mistake for something that is insensitive or illegal to be published on a website.

There is legal precedence in the EU for content of an RSS feed to land the website displaying it to be held accountable. And in UK common law, there is a ruling that suggests you don’t need to be the content producer to be responsible.

Therefore, RSS feeds still need management.

Another problem is that some people use RSS for content curation, taking content protected by copyright law and using it as their own. This can cause the person taking the content, which could be the person displaying the RSS feed to the public.

## What Precautions Can be Taken for RSS Feeds?

RSS feeds need to be carefully chosen. You can’t just use any content on them. Each website that you’re taking information from could have specific uses. They might have some terms and conditions that express how they would like to have the data used.

Another factor is that you can’t use the content on their to fill content schedules on your website. Instead, you need to look at how you can use it to bring people to you.

Some brands use RSS feeds to get people to come to their website and then add advertisements to monetise efforts.

## Final Word: 

RSS feeds might seem to be a little innocent, but they can be one of the biggest issues. Content on an RSS is not free to use. Instead, it is being marketed, at a relative free cost. It is from the RSS feed publisher to ensure that all content being displayed remains legal.
