## **How to use Analytics with Google Ads**

Google Ads is Google's advertising platform that allows businesses to create text and display advertisements. Text ads appear next to Google search results by matching keywords that you can bid on with search queries entered by users. Display ads are advertisements that consist of text, images, animation, or video that appear on the Google Display Network, which is a large collection of websites.

![How to use Analytics with Google Ads](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/WhatsApp-Image-2021-10-15-at-12.09.38-AM-300x291.jpeg)

Since the Google Store wants to sell t-shirts, they could bid on keywords such as “Google t-shirt” and “Google clothing.” When people search Google for a particular product like “a really cool Google t-shirt,” Google Ads will show a relevant text ad for the Google Store if the ad meets Google Ad's quality guidelines. This type of advertising can help attract customers from the millions that use Google Search and the Display Network every day.

When you link your Google Analytics account to your Google Ads account, you can:

-   view Google Ads click and cost data alongside your site engagement data in Google Analytics;
-   create remarketing lists in Analytics to use in Google Ads campaigns;
-   import Analytics goals and transactions into Google Ads as conversions;
-   and view Analytics site engagement data in Google Ads.

To set up a Google Ads account, follow the steps outlined in the link at the end of this lesson. It should only take you about twenty minutes.

To connect Google Analytics and Google Ads, make sure you're signed into Analytics with the same email address as your Google Ads account. Your email address can be found in the upper right corner of the page. It is important to note that you must be an administrator on both accounts.

Then, select the Admin tab. Then, ensure that you've selected the account and property that you want to link to your Google Ads account. Select "Google Ads linking" from the Property section. Click the "New link group" button. Any Google Ads accounts that you have linked to your Google account will be displayed automatically. Check the box next to the account you want to link and then click "continue." Then, enter a "Link Group Title." This could be the ID for your Google Ads account.

Select the view where you want the Google Ads data to appear and then click "Link accounts." The linked account will appear with the title you entered in your Link group list.

![link your Google Analytics account to your Google Ads account](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Link-your-Google-Analytics-account-to-your-Google-Ads-account-300x267.png)

#### **Auto-tagging**

When you link your Google Analytics and Ads accounts, campaign data is shared between the two, but campaign tracking is still required. Although you can add campaign tracking tags to Google Ads URLs manually using the URL Builder, there is a better way. With the auto-tagging feature, Google Ads can automatically add a campaign tag to your Google Ads URLs. To obtain specific Google Ads dimensions in Google Analytics, auto-tagging is required. Here are some Google Ads dimension examples:

-   Query match type shows how a Google Ads keyword is matched to a user search query.
-   Ad Group shows the ad group associated with the keyword/creative and click.
-   Destination URL shows the Google Ads destination URL configured in your Google Ads ads.
-   Ad Format describes whether the ad is a text ad, display ad, or video.
-   Ad Distribution Network shows the network used to deliver your ad.
-   Placement Domain is the domain on the content network where your ad was displayed.
-   And Google Ads Customer ID is the unique ID assigned to your Google Ads account.

With all of this data, you can conduct a more thorough analysis of your Google Adwords campaigns. Using the Ad Format dimension, for example, you can quickly compare the performance of various ad formats. You can also fine-tune your keyword matching strategy by analyzing keyword performance by match type. It is important to note that these additional dimensions and reporting features are only available if your Google Analytics and Google Ads accounts are linked.

Once we've linked Google Ads and Analytics, you'll find Ads reports under the Acquisition menu in the left-hand navigation.

#### **Campaigns**

If we click on the “Campaigns” report, we can see how well our various Google Ads campaigns are performing. Notice that this report organizes Google Ads campaigns using the names assigned in Google Ads. This is one of the benefits of linking Google Ads with Analytics.

![Campaigns](https://academy.lovetheidea.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2021/10/Campaigns-300x267.png)

Note at the top of the report, you can choose mobile, desktop, or tablet metrics to view the campaign performance across these devices. In the data table below, you can use the Acquisition metrics to see how the clicks for each campaign and the total amount paid for those clicks. CPC shows the average cost for each click. Under Behavior, you can see user engagement for each campaign. And under Conversions, you can see the conversion rate, the number of actual goal completions, and how much these conversions were ultimately worth to your business for each Google Ads campaign using the pulldown menu.

#### **Keywords**

Let's take a look at the "Keywords" report now. This can assist you in determining how well your keywords and individual ads are performing. For example, if a keyword generates a lot of traffic but has a high bounce rate, this could indicate a mismatch between the ad and the landing page content. When a keyword with a high conversion rate has a low number of impressions (or the number of times an ad was shown), you may want to increase your bid for that keyword to increase the number of times your ad is displayed. You can add "Device Category" as another dimension to separate these keywords based on the type of device the users were using when they clicked your ad and visited your site.

#### **Bid adjustments**

Finally, let's take a look at the "Bid adjustments" report. A Google Ads feature that adjusts keyword bids based on a user's device, location, or time of day is bid adjustments. For example, if Google Store opens a temporary location to sell merchandise during the holidays, they may want to include a bid adjustment to increase ad visibility on mobile devices within three miles of the store during operating hours.

The Bid Adjustment report in Analytics allows you to examine Google Ads performance in relation to the bid adjustments you've set for your campaigns. You can evaluate campaign performance by device, location, time of day, and remarketing list bid adjustments using the selector at the top of the table. In an advanced course, we'll go over remarketing. Click on the campaign name in the list to see all of your bid adjustments and metrics for that campaign.

You can see how effective Google Ads can be for your company when combined with Google Analytics. It enables you to assess the worth of your marketing and make changes to improve your return on investment.
